# Recording correspondence

Before proof work, compare each proposed formal statement against the pinned paper. Record its objects, inherited hypotheses, ranges, and every conclusion. Track missing conclusions as subproofs. Repeat with the checked declaration before marking the automated comparison complete.

## Independent readings

Use the agent running this skill to orchestrate two isolated LLM readings. Give the paper reader only the manuscript passage, edition and surrounding assumptions. Give the other reader the exact expected Lean statement, its definitions and pin, with no paper text, title-based hints or prewritten paper summary. Ask each for mathematical language that a non-Lean author can verify and precise source references. When workers are available, launch the two readers in separate contexts. If independent readings are unavailable, keep comparison pending and continue authorized proof work.

Run `atlas.py review-inputs PROJECT CLAIM --side paper` and `--side lean` separately to collect the evidence. The Lean side includes `defaults`: each place where the statement's definitions use an operation Lean makes total with a default value (x/0 = 0, log 0 = 0, sInf ∅ = 0, truncated ℕ subtraction, and the junk values of limits, sums, integrals and derivatives). The Lean reading addresses each listed use in one line: why the default cannot arise under the hypotheses, or what it means if it does. Carry those lines into the comparison so the author reviews them. The paper reader can inspect the manuscript at the cited lines; the Lean reader can inspect the audited modules. The coordinator compares objects, quantifiers, assumptions, ranges and all conclusions. Before recording a match, have an LLM check the exact displayed Lean reading against the formal type and cited definitions. Correct omitted quantifiers or mistranslated terms and repeat this check. Preserve distinctions such as one-sided versus two-sided bounds, and state meaningful extra conclusions. Automated agreement is evidence, not a guarantee.

## Record or revise the assessment

Write the comparison for an author who understands the mathematics and may have never read Lean. Use the existing independent readings when their bindings still match. Check the formal declaration and definitions and the manuscript passage before writing the justification. Break the claim at mathematically meaningful cases. Show the short identity, implication, counterexample, or endpoint calculation that explains each verdict. Distinguish a stronger formal conclusion from a missing paper obligation. Identify where an apparent equivalence depends on interpreting a word such as “first” or “at.” Cite the paper lines and named Lean definitions that let the author check your reasoning.

Every new comparison record includes an `analysis` object with these fields:

- `summary` gives the verdict in the language of this theorem. Say explicitly when the checked theorem implies the paper claim and also proves more. For a stronger result, identify the direction of implication, the extra clauses, and the existence or absence of a separately checked paper-facing corollary.
- `cases` is a nonempty array of objects with four strings: `case`, `paper`, `lean`, and `why`. A single row is enough for a single implication.
- `scope` says what follows, including any meaningful extra conclusion or missing case.
- `advice` explains which decision fits the evidence and why. For a conditional recommendation, state the condition the author should check.
- `suggested_feedback` gives editable wording in the author's voice for a difference or open question. Include it for `difference` or `unsure`, and optionally for a plausible alternative interpretation of a `match`. Never imply the author has supplied or accepted the wording.

When the checked theorem covers the entire paper claim and has a meaningful extra conclusion, optionally add a top-level `stronger_result` string on the comparison record. State exactly what is extra and where it applies. This adds a visible **Lean proves more** note on the claim and a link from the dashboard. A missing paper obligation still requires a `difference` or `unsure` assessment. A reviewer may attach a separate, attributed scope note to an approval.

Generate this analysis as part of the coding agent's normal comparison step. The browser and static build display the saved assessment without calling an external model. When reopening an older atlas, fill in missing analyses on current checked claims before inviting a new approval. Changing an analysis changes the version presented for approval. Earlier decisions retain their original evidence in review history.

Create a JSON object containing the unmodified `bindings` returned by the command, two nonempty strings `paper_reading` and `lean_reading`, corresponding `paper_refs` and `lean_refs`, and `result` equal to `match`, `difference`, or `unsure`. For the latter two also supply `issue`, `actor` (Proof agent or Author), and `next` (what will close the gap). Submit it with `atlas.py compare PROJECT CLAIM --readings FILE`. The command validates the bindings and updates comparisons.json and the atlas. Reuse the recorded bindings only for the evidence actually read.

For feedback from a reviewer, inspect review history and the `reported_differences` IDs from `review-inputs` without --side. After investigating, include the addressed IDs as a `resolves` array and a specific `resolution`. Retain already resolved IDs when updating the assessment. Reconcile an incorrect display with the audited formal statement, complete a missing bound, derive the paper claim from a stronger one, or quarantine a proof of a different theorem under its correct scope. For genuine ambiguities show competing readings. Ask the author for a mathematical decision when evidence calls for one. Pin a revised manuscript as a new edition and compare again.

## Author decisions and release

At every review checkpoint inspect `reviews.json` and the **Manuscript edits** list. Route a mathematical mismatch to correspondence. Extract actionable manuscript requests from ordinary comments even when the author approved the theorem. Treat a clarification compatible with the claimed mathematics as an independent editorial suggestion. Propose exact old/new text against the pinned source and link it to the originating review event. The dedicated manuscript-edit field appears in the list immediately. Consolidate repeated suggestions by referencing their source rather than creating duplicate tasks.

Store curated items under `{"version":1,"edits":{...}}` in `manuscript-edits.json`. Use a stable key (the first review event ID for an edit originating there), and fields `claim`, `edition`, `location`, `original`, `replacement`, `reason`, `status` and optional `review_id`. Read the latest ledger under `author_review.project_lock` and atomically update only the intended entry. Preserve the separate `cleared` map and human choices. A draft request authorizes preparing a reviewable manuscript patch. Locate the editable source and confirm the quoted text still matches. Record the draft path or URL in `evidence` with status `Draft ready`. Integrate or publish a manuscript revision only when separately authorized, then record its revision as `Applied`. Do not edit a pinned paper snapshot in place. A cleared suggestion stays in history and should not be recreated on resume. Check local publication intent before sharing private review records.

Start `atlas.py review PROJECT` for the author. The CLI alternative is `atlas.py approve PROJECT --by NAME`. These paths share the same evidence and decisions. Launching review is authorized when requested; approving on someone’s behalf is not. Never manufacture reviews.json. On resume, inspect outstanding feedback and continue within the authorized scope. Report saved-local versus published state accurately. Before publishing include the intended comparisons.json and reviews.json in the checked commit and verify the public record after deployment. Self-reported reviewer names do not authenticate an identity. Static Pages exposes evidence; local review collects decisions.
